All runes can be purchased as single use items from stores, runes are drawn on Runic Material, explained lower down. They can then be used for enchanting and spells. Runes cannot be activated without a level in Runemaster path. 
# Runic Inscription (Enchanting)
Runes are ancient symbols etched into items to tap into the fundamental forces of the world.
- Xeran Runes: Used for Permanent Enchantments. Once applied, the item is permanently changed.
- Vindroth Runes: Used for complex Spellcasting. Will be explained lower down.
### Runic Logic & Constraints
- A single Xeran rune can be etched onto an item with no penalty. However, if you wish to place two or more effect runes on a single object, they must be bound within a Runic Circle.
- Enchanted items cannot be modified. You must decide the full runic array before the first inscription.
- A single runic circle can support a maximum of 10 Effect Runes.
- Combine runes compress their consumed runes: a Combine and the two elementals it uses count as 1 effective rune. A Greater Combine consumes two Combine/Greater results (removing them from the count by 2) and adds itself as 1, for a net reduction of 1. A True Combine counts itself as 1, and each rune or result it consumes still counts individually. Carbon runes are exempt from the count.
- The Carbon Ratio: In any circle, the volatile energies must be balanced by Carbon Runes. The formula is:
	- *Number of Carbon Runes = Number of Effect Runes - 1*

## The Inscription Ritual
Inscribing a runic array is a delicate process of channeling mana through physical matter. To successfully enchant an item, a crafter must perform a sequence of Inscription Checks, one for every rune in the array (including Carbon runes).

**The Ritual Sequence**
- If you are only inscribing a single rune you can skip this and move to the Inscription check. 
- If you are creating a Runic Circle, you must roll for every rune in the circle, pick the first rune to inscribe (it doesn't matter which one) and then go around the circle at your leisure, whichever rune you start with will be considered the Starting rune for Combine rune purposes. Mana is spent as you go. You must outline the entire circle and put a blank or marker space for each rune before you begin the inscription, you cannot change which runes you will be using after the inscription starts. 
- If you pass the roll, the rune is successfully etched. Move to the next rune in the array. There is no time limit in between runic checks, you may wait as long as you need before attempting to inscribe the next rune as long as the circle remains largely undistrubed. Until all runes in the circle are inscribed the runes will do nothing. 
- **Critical Success (Natural 20**): The rune is etched perfectly; you gain Advantage on the next roll in the sequence and no mana is spent on this rune.
- **Carbon Runes**: Carbon runes are required if inscribing a Runic Circle or anything with more than 1 Effect Rune. Carbon runes may be placed anywhere on the front or end of the circle and are grouped together. Depending on the situation you may want to inscribe them first or last but they must always be placed together and either done all at once first or all at once last. 

**The Inscription Check**
- For each rune, roll a d20 and add your Relevant Affinity Modifier.
- The Base DC is 12 and it goes up depending on the DC Mod (see above table) and up or down depending on the [[2.6.1 Metals and Stones Materials|Materials]] Enchantability.
	- **Example**: Inscribing a Fire Rune has a total DC of 16, if inscribing onto a Copper plate for example you would reduce the DC by 4 to 12 beacuse it has an Enchantability of 4.
- When you make the check for the rune you must spend its mana cost. The cost is calculated by the cost of the rune and the size of the circle (the Mana Surcharge).
	- **Example**: The mana cost for a Fire rune in a 3 rune circle would be 4 (from the rune itself) + 10 (for the mana surcharge). This mana will be spent for EACH rune in the circle after a successful check is made. You do not need to spend any mana on the carbon runes but they still require a check. 
	- So in the prevous example the circle would be ```[Fire] [Fire] [Fire] [Carbon] [Carbon]```. This would require 5 seperate rolls, the first three being at a DC of 12 with a mana cost of 14 per run and the last two being at a DC of 8 with no cost. 

**Failed Inscriptions**
- If a single roll in the sequence fails the ritual fails and the item becomes saturated with unstable mana, thej mana cost from the Inscription check is still spent, but the rune is still ruined.
- The item cannot be enchanted further, and the current runes provide no bonuses and are destroyed.
- To clear the Saturated state you must spend some mana to fix the item, the mana cost will change based on how much mana you spent so far using the below formula. Once fixed, the item is restored to its original, non-magical state and can be enchanted again from scratch, ALL runes are destroyed in this process. The Desaturation cost can be spent overtime, as long as all mana is paid. The runes will be completley unusable until all mana is paid. For each day the runes have been saturated the desaturation cost raises by 20% (rounding down) up to its desaturation cost. This means in order to successfully desaturate an item over time you must spend greater than 20% of its desaturation cost each day. The cost will never raise past its initial cost. 
	- **Desaturation formula:** 1 + (Mana Spent / 3 (rounding down)) x (Number of Non-Carbon Runes Successfully Inscribed + 5)
	- **Example:** Say you successfully inscribed 2 of 3 fire runes in a runic circle that was meant to be 3 fire runes. 
		- The cost of one Fire rune inscription would be 4 (from the rune itself) + 10 (for the mana surcharge) so that's 14 mana per rune. We had two successues (28) plus the failed rune (42) so we spent 42 mana. 
		- Divide the mana we spent by 3 (rounding down), 42 / 3 = 14 and add 1, 15. 
		- Now multiply the mana cost by the number of runes successfully inscribed plus 5. 2 runes + 5 = 7. This would be 15 x 7 = 105.
		- So in order to desaturate the item/inscription you would need to spend 105 mana over the course of any amount of time. 
	- **Note:** Carbon runes have no mana cost so they are not included in this, if you failed and have only done checks on Carbon runes and nothing else it will cost nothing to desaturate it. 
## Making New Elements
To use Affinities other than the basic ones you must combine the basic ones to make more powerful ones. A Combine rune merges the two elemental runes that come immediately before it, going counterclockwise through the circle. The two elemental runes must already be inscribed and their mana cost paid. The Combine rune looks up the pair in [[2.1 Affinities]]: if any affinity's unlock requirement matches those two elements, the combination succeeds and the circle now deals the combined element's damage type and the combined dice from the used runes instead of the individual components. If no affinity matches, the Combine rune automatically fails on inscription and ruins the circle. The Combine rune uses the resulting affinity for its inscription check. The Runemaster does not have to have that affinity unlocked in order to make these roles but will have a -5 pentality to the inscription roll if they do not. A Greater Combine rune works the same way, but acts on the results of Combine or Greater Combine runes. It looks counterclockwise for the next two Combine or Greater Combine runes, skips past the elemental runes those Combines point to, and uses their resulting elements as its two inputs. Greater and regular elemental runes will not combine together. True Combine consumes everything to the left (counterclockwise) of it in the circle to the start of the circle that has not already been claimed by a Combine or Greater Combine, including elemental runes and any Combine/Greater Combine results, and checks the full set against [[2.1 Affinities]]. If any affinity's unlock matches, the circle deals that element and the insrcription fails otherwise. Combine runes will reduce the number of Effect runes in the circle by 2 but still count as a rune themselves, however they and the effect runes they "consume" still need 1 carbon rune each. This means that although something like Lightning would have Fire, Air, Combine and need 2 carbon runes it would still only have 1 Effect rune meaning the surcharge cost and level requirement is only at level 1 instead of level 3. 
#### Examples
**Lightning (Fire + Air)**
```
[Fire] [Air] [Combine] [Carbon] [Carbon]
```
1. Counterclockwise from Combine: Air then Fire. Fire + Air = Lightning. The circle deals Lightning damage with the dice from both runes (2d6 Lightning instead of 1d6 Fire + 1d6 Air).
	- Note that you must have already paid for the inscription for the Fire and Air before doing the Combine. 
	- **Effective Runes**: 1. The Combine and its two elementals count as 1 effect rune, so the circle uses 1 of 10 slots. 

**Obsidian (Magma + Water)**
Obsidian requires Magma and Water. Magma is Fire + Earth.
```
[Fire] [Earth] [Combine] [Water] [Greater Combine] [Carbon] x4
```
1. First Combine (counterclockwise): Earth then Fire = Magma (2d6). Greater Combine looks counterclockwise uses Water then the Combine result (Magma). Magma (2d6) + Water (1d6) = Obsidian (3d6). 
	- **Effective runes**: 1. Combine condenses Fire and Earth to 1, Greater condenses Water and the Combine result to 1.

**Blight (Nature + Corruption)**
Nature = Dust + Water + Radiant. Corruption = Radiant + Necrotic.
```
[Air] [Earth] [Combine(A)] [Water] [Radiant] [True Combine] [Radiant] [Necrotic] [Combine(B)] [Greater Combine] [Carbon] x9
```
Inscribe:
1. Air (1d6) and Earth (1d6), then Combine(A) = Dust (2d6)
2. Water (1d6) and Radiant (1d6), then True Combine: collects everything unused Elemental and Combines back to the start, Water, Radiant, and the Dust result. Dust + Water + Radiant matches Nature's unlock = Nature (4d6)
3. Radiant (1d6) and Necrotic (1d6), then Combine(B) = Corruption (2d6)
4. Greater Combine: looks counter clock wise for two Combine results, sees Combine(B)/Corruption and True/Nature. Nature + Corruption = Blight (6d6).
	- **Effective Runes**: 4. Combine(A) condenses Air and Earth into 1 effective rune for Dust. Water and Radiant are unused at this point, and True Combine does not compress its consumed runes, so they add 3 more for a running total of 4. Combine(B) condenses Radiant and Necrotic to 1, bringing the total to 5. Greater Combine then consumes Combine(A) and Combine(B), removing both from the count and adding itself, dropping the total back to 4.

**Miasma (Necrotic + Poison + Toxin + Blight)**
```
[Air] [Earth] [Combine(A)] [Water] [Radiant] [True Combine (A)] [Radiant] [Necrotic] [Combine(B)] [Greater Combine] [Necrotic] [Water] [Combine(C)] [Necrotic] [Air] [Combine(D)] [Necrotic] [True Combine (B)] [Carbon] x17
```
Inscribe counterclockwise:
1. Air (1d6) and Earth (1d6), then Combine(A) = Dust (2d6)
2. Water (1d6) and Radiant (1d6), then True Combine: collects Water, Radiant, and Dust. Dust + Water + Radiant = Nature (4d6)
3. Radiant (1d6) and Necrotic (1d6), then Combine(B) = Corruption (2d6)
4. Greater Combine: Nature (4d6) + Corruption (2d6) = Blight (6d6)
5. Necrotic (1d6) and Water (1d6), then Combine(C) = Poison (2d6)
6. Necrotic (1d6) and Air (1d6), then Combine(D) = Toxin (2d6)
7. Inscribe the last Necrotic (1d6), then True Combine (B): collects everything unused counterclockwise to the start, the Blight result (6d6), Poison result (2d6), Toxin result (2d6), and the remaining Necrotic rune (1d6). Necrotic + Poison + Toxin + Blight matches Miasma's unlock. The circle deals 11d6 Miasma damage.
	- **Effective Runes**: 8. Combine(A) condenses Air and Earth to 1 effective rune for Dust. True(A) consumes Water, Radiant, and the Dust result without compressing them, adding 3 for a total of 4. Combine(B) condenses Radiant and Necrotic to 1 effective rune for Corruption, bringing it to 5. Greater Combine consumes Combine(A) (Nature) and Combine(B) (Corruption), removing both and adding itself, dropping back to 4 effective runes, now as Blight. Combine(C) (Poison) and Combine(D) (Toxin) each condense their pairs to 1, raising the total to 6. True(B) consumes Blight, Poison, Toxin, and the remaining Necrotic without compressing them, adding 2 for a final count of 8.

**Quantum (Atomic + Corruption)**
Atomic = Generic + Fire + Earth + Water + Air + Radiant + Necrotic. Corruption = Radiant + Necrotic.
```
[Generic] [Fire] [Earth] [Water] [Air] [Radiant] [Necrotic] [True(A)] [Radiant] [Necrotic] [Combine(B)] [Greater] [Reduce] [Reduce] [Carbon] x13
```
Inscribe counterclockwise:
1. Generic (1d6), Fire (1d6), Earth (1d6), Water (1d6), Air (1d6), Radiant (1d6), and Necrotic (1d6), then True(A): collects all seven unused elementals and checks against the affinities table to get Atomic (7d6). 
2. Radiant (1d6) and Necrotic (1d6), then Combine(B): these two elementals are consumed and compressed into 1 effective rune. Radiant + Necrotic matches Corruption (2d6).
3. Greater Combine: looks counterclockwise, sees Combine(B)/Corruption and True(A)/Atomic. Atomic + Corruption = Quantum. The circle deals 9d6 Quantum damage.
	- **Effective Runes**: Without Reduce, this circle would have 8 effective runes. True(A) consumes seven elementals, each still counting individually under True for a total of 8. Combine(B) adds 1 for 9. Greater consumes True(A) and Combine(B), removing both and adding itself, dropping back to 8. Each Reduce rune subtracts 1 from the final count, so with two Reduce runes the circle finishes at 6 effective runes.
	- **Mana Surcharge**: At 6 effective runes the base surcharge is +50 mana per rune. Two Reduce runes add +100% (50% each, additive), raising the surcharge to +100 mana. Each rune in the circle costs its own mana cost plus 100 from the surcharge, so a Necrotic rune (base cost 4) would cost 104 mana to inscribe. This is substantial savings: without the Reduce runes the circle would have 8 effective runes and a surcharge of +175 per rune. The two Reduce runes cut 75 mana from every rune in the circle, saving 975 mana across 13 non-carbon runes while only costing 300 mana to inscribe the Reduce runes themselves, a net gain of 675 mana. The level requirement also drops from 4 to 2.
# Vindroth Runes
Vindroth runes function like a programmed trigger: when a condition is met, an effect occurs. They are inscribed as ordered words that form a readable sequence (ex: If … Than … Else …) rather than in circles. Where Xeran runes create permanent enchantments, Vindroth runes create conditional spells that respond to the world around them.
### Inscription Rules
- Each Vindroth rune occupies 1 square inch of space.
- All runes in a paragraph must be inscribed into one continuous piece of material.
- If the material has a complete break that separates words, the paragraph loses the separated words. This may weaken, change, or fully disable the effect.
### Vindroth Structure Limits
A Vindroth paragraph is built from two resources: paragraph slots and conditional slots. **Than** is always free and does not consume any slot.

**Paragraph slots** control how many If or While blocks the paragraph can contain. You gain 1 paragraph slot at level 1 and an additional one every 2 levels thereafter (1 at level 1, 2 at level 2, 3 at level 4, 4 at level 6, and so on).

**Conditional slots** control the complexity of each block. You gain 1 conditional slot per level, starting at 1 at level 1 (1 at level 1, 2 at level 2, 3 at level 3, and so on). Conditional slots are spent on everything that specifies or modifies what is being checked or how the paragraph flows. This includes:
- **Condition modifiers**: object, self, touched, entered, within, size, shape, has, lacks, Not, at, moved, attacked, damaged, healed, cast
- **Condition connectors**: And, Or (used inside an If or While to require multiple things or alternatives)
- **Flow control**: Else (defines an alternate result), And (chains an additional result after the first)

The result itself (the text after Than or Else) does not consume a conditional slot. For example, Power (Xeran Fire) or Heal (Vitality) are free, only the And that chains multiple results together costs a slot.

**Global parameters** are runes placed above the paragraph, before any If or While. They apply to the entire spell. The first global rune is free; each additional global rune must be chained with And, which costs a conditional slot. Only effect runes which change the effect of the paragraph (Speed Up, Speed Down, etc.) may be placed globally.

### Using Xeran as Effect Payloads
Vindroth effects can call on a single Xeran rune or an entire Xeran circle. A circle allows combined elements and complex effects without needing to cram everything into the paragraph itself. For example, Than Power (Xeran Circle: [Fire] [Air] [Combine] [Carbon] [Carbon]) deals Lightning damage. The circle is inscribed as normal and follows all Xeran rules including Carbon requirements, effective rune limits, and mana costs.

**Example structure**:
- If object (human) entered size (5) shape (sphere) Than Power (Xeran Fire)
To increase output, repeat the effect with And:
- … Than Power (Xeran Fire) And Power (Xeran Fire)
### Inscription Ritual
Inscribing a Vindroth paragraph follows a similar process to Xeran circles but is more forgiving: each rune stands alone and a failure only ruins the individual rune rather than the entire paragraph.

**The Ritual Sequence**
- Write the paragraph in order from left to right. You must outline all rune positions before beginning and cannot add or remove runes once you start. Each rune must be inscribed into one solid continuous material. 
- For each rune, make an Inscription Check. If you pass, the rune is etched and you move to the next one. If you fail, that rune is destroyed and the mana spent on it is lost, but all previously inscribed runes remain intact. You may replace the failed rune with a new one and attempt it again, continuing from where you left off.
- There is no time limit between checks as long as the paragraph remains undisturbed. Until every rune in the paragraph is successfully inscribed, the paragraph does nothing.
- **Critical Success (Natural 20):** The rune is etched perfectly. You gain Advantage on the next roll and no mana is spent on this rune.

**The Inscription Check**
- For each rune, roll a d20 and add your Generic Attack Roll Bonus to the roll.
- The DC is 12 + the rune's DC Mod. Materials with Enchantability reduce this DC as normal (see [[2.6.1 Metals and Stones Materials]]).
- When you succeed, spend the rune's Mana Cost. Unlike Xeran circles there is no surcharge per rune: each Vindroth rune costs exactly its listed Mana Cost.

**Failed Inscriptions**
- If a check fails, only that rune is destroyed. All previously inscribed runes remain intact and functional. The mana spent on the failed rune is lost.
- You may replace the failed rune with a fresh copy of the same rune and attempt it again. There is no penalty for doing so beyond the cost of the replacement rune and the mana spent on the new attempt.

### Conveyor Belt
A belt system using two paragraph types. The Starter tile detects objects and pushes them forward. The Belt tile is infinitely repeatable on consecutive tiles.

# Making Your Own Runes
In order to make your own runes you must first be at least a level 5 Runemaster. See [[3.3 Leveling Up]] for more. Rather than buying runes from a store you can make them yourself. The result is a single-use Xeran or Vindroth rune identical to a store-bought one. It can be sold, traded, or used in the Inscription Ritual.
### Materials
You need a Blank Runic Medium and Affinity Ink. See [[2.6.3 Special and Other Materials#Manufactured]] for pricing or make them yourself using the rules below.
##### Making Blank Runic Medium
1. Take any material with an Enchantability of 5 or greater (see [[2.6.1 Metals and Stones Materials]]). You need about 2 oz.
2. Flatten the material so it has at least 1 square inch of space to draw on, the flattening process will depend on the material you chose but they are typically metal so they can usually be forged.
3. Imbue it with 25 mana and make a DC 12 Arcana check. If you succeed it is now a Blank Runic Medium. On failure the material is destroyed.
##### Making Affinity Ink
1. Take 1 oz of crushed obsidian, voidsand, or void slime mass.
2. Imbue it with 25 mana and make a DC 12 Arcana check. If you succeed it is now Affinity Ink. On failure the material is destroyed.
### Manufacturing The Rune
Once you have a Blank Runic Medium and Affinity Ink, follow these steps:

1. **Draw the Rune.** Use your Affinity Ink to draw the rune onto the Blank Runic Medium. It does not matter what you draw with (a feather, a stick, your finger, etc). To draw the rune you must know what it looks like. You know what it looks like if you have another copy of it you can see or if you have successfully inscribed or made it 10 or more times. Make a Sleight of Hand check (DC 15). If you have another rune next to you to copy and have inscribed or made it 10+ times you have advantage on this roll.

2. **Activate the Rune.** Spend the rune's Mana Cost. You may add your Runic DC Check bonus (from Rune Master Level 3 / 3.5) to this roll.

**Success:** You create a fully functional rune.
**Failure:** The materials and mana are consumed and destroyed.
