## The Physical World: Plains and Ether
The universe of Eyum is not a sphere of rock orbiting a star. Every deity's domain is a flat, circular plain of solid matter, constantly moving upward through a medium called Ether. A plain is a disc (or more of a short cylinder) of land, water, and whatever else the deity has shaped, surrounded at its border by a wall, most commonly ice, that prevents anything from falling or blowing off the edge. Eyum itself is the plain of Darwin, the Ancient of Earth and Rock, and it is where all mortal life exists.

Ether is a weightless, transparent, frictionless substance that permeates all matter and all space. It cannot be detected chemically or physically by normal means. Ether is the medium through which all light, sound, waves, and force propagate. There is no such thing as a true vacuum in the scientific sense (other than void itself) because Ether fills everything, though the spellcasting affinity called Vacuum creates zones absent of air, not absent of Ether, and Void is the true absence of Ether itself. Ether is what pushes objects toward the surface of a plain: a plain flies upward constantly, and the thick resistance of Ether against that motion pushes everything downward. This is gravity. A falling object is not being pulled down, it is being overtaken by the upward rush of Ether as the plain moves past it. 

If you fall over the edge of a plain, you do not continue falling. You stay where you are, suspended in Ether, while the plain continues moving upward away from you. If you can fly upward, you will eventually catch the planet again as it rises past you (if you can fly fast enough, the rate at which each disk moves is different per disk). Above the plains there is no ceiling, you can fly upward indefinitely, but there is nothing up there except more Ether and, at vast distances, the lights of other plains visible as stars. Every star, planet, moon, and asteroid in the night sky is another deity's personal domain, a plain of its own moving through the Ether at its own level and distance. Some are close enough to be resolved as discs, most are too far away to be anything but points of light. All of them are moving "upward" forever.
### Ether Sight
Certain creatures and spells can grant Ether Sight, a vision type that reveals the Ether itself. With Ether Sight active, the world takes on a faint pink haze. This haze represents the ambient Ether flowing through all space and matter. The primary use of Ether Sight is detecting Void: wherever Ether is absent, the pink haze drops away to a sharp, pitch-black void, and you can see Ether visibly flowing into the hole like water down a drain. Ether Sight imposes a -1 penalty to all Search and Spot checks because the pink overlay adds visual noise to everything and you cannot see anything past your Ether sight range. See [[1.6 Other Rules#Types of Vision]] for more. 
## The Planes of Existence
Layered on top of every physical plain, occupying the same space but separated by dimensional boundaries, are five planes of existence. Where the physical plains are separate locations separated by distance, the planes of existence are stacked directly on top of each other, occupying the same coordinates but in different dimensions. Each one exists on every physical plain. A creature on Darwin's Eyum and a creature on the plain of another deity are in different physical locations, but they share the same five planes of existence layered over both of them. Each plane of existence is ruled by a specific deity who has near-total control over what happens within it on their own physical plain, and partial influence over it on the plains of other deities. Some planes exist for a specific cosmic purpose, others are simply byproducts of how the universe was structured.

**Isthmus: The Mortal Realm**
Isthmus is the plane of existence that mortals inhabit by default. It has no single ruling deity, it is the shared space where all physical life happens. Isthmus is normal. There is nothing mechanically distinct about being in Isthmus because it is the baseline from which all other planes are measured. Every creature that is not explicitly in another plane of existence is in Isthmus.

**Speculum: The Still World**
Speculum is a parallel dimension that mirrors Isthmus exactly, the geography is identical, every building, tree, mountain, and river exists in both planes at the same coordinates, but nothing in Speculum moves or changes (unless moved in Isthmus first, it always mirrors Isthmus). There are no people, no animals, no living creatures of any kind (other than plants which are more like petrified copies of their living counterparts), and objects in Speculum are frozen in place. It looks exactly like Isthmus but completely empty and silent. Speculum is ruled by the Fiend, Screaming Hymn.

When any creature becomes intangible through an ability like Shade Slip (see [[4.0 Races/Undead and Subraces]]), Phase Walk (see [[6.1.2 Unique, Racial, and Healing Spells#Unique Spells]]), the Ghost modifier (see [[7.3.5 Monster Modifiers]]), or any other source of intangibility, that creature partially enters Speculum. "Partially" means they exist in both planes at once: their physical form is in Speculum where it can pass through frozen copies of walls and objects, but their senses remain anchored in Isthmus so they can still see, hear, and perceive creatures there. This is why intangible creatures treat solid objects as difficult terrain, they are not truly passing through the Isthmus version of the object; they are walking through its empty, frozen Speculum counterpart while still navigating by Isthmus coordinates.

Because Speculum is empty, a creature fully in Speculum cannot interact with anything except the terrain. They cannot open doors, pick up items, pull levers, attack creatures, or cast spells that require a physical target in Isthmus. They can move freely through any space that is not occupied by a living creature in Isthmus. See [[1.6 Other Rules#Moving Through Objects and Displacement]] for what happens if they end their turn inside an occupied space.

Speculum is not dangerous by itself. The main risk of being there is getting displaced back into Isthmus inside a solid object. Screaming Hymn can theoretically interact with creatures in Speculum but almost never does, they rule over Speculum mostly only in name. Not through some form of actual dominion but more because none of the other deities bother.

**Penumbra: The Void**
Penumbra is a plane of pure void. There is no ground, no Ether, no light, no sound, no gravity, and no sense of direction. It is an infinite black emptiness. Within it float scattered objects, debris, and the occasional unfortunate creature, but because Penumbra is infinite and these objects are randomly distributed, the probability of encountering anything else while in Penumbra is effectively zero. Penumbra is ruled by the Ancient, Hawking, whose affinity is Void, the direct opposite of Ether. Void is the true absence of Ether so as such there is no form of gravity in the Penumbra.

Penumbra is deadly to normal creatures. Any creature in Penumbra gains the Suffocating condition immediately, as there is no breathable air. Additionally, a creature in Penumbra takes 5 Void damage at the start of each of its turns. Creatures with the Void Touched condition do not take this Void damage but still suffer from Suffocating normally unless they do not need to breathe. Creatures with both Void Touched and a source of air or water breathing can survive in Penumbra indefinitely, though they still have no way to navigate or orient themselves.

There is no movement in Penumbra in the traditional sense. Because there is no frame of reference and no gravity, a creature in Penumbra cannot walk, run, or fly in a chosen direction. They simply float, suspended indefinitely in the void. A creature may move by pushing itself off of something else or by throwing an object away from them, there is no friction so they will continue moving in that direction endlessly. Flight and movement spells do not work in Penumbra. If you have no objects around you then you cannot move. The only reliable way to exit Penumbra is through magic, specifically, spells that shift a creature between planes of existence.

**Moving in Penumbra:** Flight and movement spells do not work in Penumbra and you cannot walk, run, or fly since there is nothing to push against and no gravity. The only way to move is by pushing off an object or throwing something away from you. There is no friction in Penumbra so any movement you gain continues at a constant speed in that direction until you collide with something or push off another object. Pushing off an additional object adds to your current speed to a maximum of twice the speed of your first push. If you have no objects to push off of or throw then you cannot move at all.

**Movement Speed Formula:** When you push off or throw an object, you and the object both move in opposite directions. The lighter of the two always moves at full base speed. The heavier one moves at a reduced speed based on how much heavier it is.

- **Your base speed:** 5 ft per point of your STR modifier (minimum 5 ft). This is not a movement speed you can use freely, it is only used when pushing off or throwing an object.
- **If you and the object weigh the same:** both move at your full base speed in opposite directions.
- **If you are lighter than the object:** you move at full base speed. The object moves at a reduced speed: (your weight / object weight) x your base speed, rounded down. If that amount is less than 5 ft, the object does not move.
- **If the object is lighter than you:** the object moves at full base speed away from you. You move at a reduced speed: (object weight / your weight) x your base speed, rounded down to a minimum of 5 ft. You always move at least 5 ft no matter how light the object, however if the object rounds up to 5 it is not additive.
- **Multi-push cap:** movement is additive, pushing off additional objects adds to your current speed up to a maximum of twice your first push's speed (unless the object is far lighter than you as stated before).
- Pushing off an object within your reach costs a bonus action. Throwing an object costs an action.

**Example:** A creature (200 lbs, STR +4, base 20 ft) pushes off a 400 lb boulder. The creature is lighter, so they move at full speed: 20 ft per round. The boulder is heavier, so it moves at (200/400) x 20 = 50% x 20 = 10 ft per round.
**Example:** A creature (200 lbs, STR +4, base 20 ft) pushes off a 40 lb rock. The rock is lighter, so it moves at full speed: 20 ft per round away from them. The creature is heavier, so they move at (40/200) x 20 = 20% x 20 = 4, rounded up to 5 ft (minimum). They drift at 5 ft per round.
**Example:** Two creatures of equal weight (both 150 lbs) push off each other with STR +3 (base 15 ft). They weigh the same, so both move at 15 ft per round in opposite directions.

**Gahenna: The Land of the Dead**
Gahenna is where the souls of the dead reside. It is ruled by Iron Pit, the deity of Infernal flame and punishment. When a sentient creature dies in Isthmus, its soul is separated from its body. Creatures that are not sentient (INT 14 or lower, and all non-humanoids) have no soul and simply cease to exist. Sentient creatures (INT 15 or higher, and all humanoids) have a soul that persists after death and is drawn to Gahenna. Souls are indestructible, they cannot be killed, or destroyed by any means, when they take damage and reach 0 they simply enter a state of nothing and then reform 1 day later with their full stats. 

The landscape of Gahenna mirrors the geography of the corresponding Isthmus plain, but it is scorched and ruined. The ground is cracked black stone. Red and purple infernal flames burn perpetually from fissures. There are no structures and no sky, above is only darkness. Souls in Gahenna retain their memories, personality, and intelligence from life but cannot gain levels, cannot grow stronger, and cannot be resurrected, although in some rare cases they can return to Isthmus. Resurrection does not exist in this cosmology. Death is final and universal.

Souls in Gahenna exist in a state of meaningless eternity. They wander, interact with each other, sometimes fight, but mostly do nothing because there is nothing to do. Over centuries or millennia, every soul eventually goes mad from the endless, purposeless existence. When a soul completely loses its intelligence and self-awareness, it is destroyed and ceases to exist. This is the ultimate fate of all souls in Gahenna. All souls in Gahenna lose 1 INT and 1 WIS score each year they exist. WIS will stop going down once it reaches 2 but when a creatures INT reaches 2 they cease to exist.

Some souls do not go to Gahenna. A creature that dies with extreme unfinished business may instead become a ghost, remaining in Isthmus as a Banshee (see [[7.3.3.1 Banshee]]) or similar incorporeal undead. A creature whose soul is bound to an object may become a Relicwraith (see [[4.0 Races/Undead and Subraces]]). In these cases the soul is trapped in Isthmus rather than moving on. A soul that is destroyed by magic in Isthmus never reaches Gahenna at all. 

**Nirvana: Ascension**
Nirvana is the destination for the most powerful souls. It is ruled by Seraphael. When any creature of level 100 or higher, or a monster of DR 100 or higher, dies, its soul ascends to Nirvana instead of descending to Gahenna. This is automatic and cannot be prevented or chosen.

Nirvana is similar to Gahenna in structure and function: a mirrored version of the Isthmus geography, but flooded with warm golden light rather than infernal fire. The sky is bright blue with streaks of gold. The ground is smooth white marble stone. Souls in Nirvana have all the same limitations as souls in Gahenna, they cannot grow, cannot return, and will eventually go mad and be destroyed from the purposelessness of eternity. The only difference is that Nirvana is peaceful and beautiful rather than painful and horrifying. For the soul experiencing it, the end result is the same. All souls in Nirvana lose 1 INT and 1 WIS score every 5 years they exist. WIS will stop going down once it reaches 2 but when a creatures INT reaches 2 they cease to exist.

**The Corrupted Planes**
Corruption, Blight, and Infection affinities are not native to Isthmus, instead they are from other plains of existence and infecting the physical plain. Each one is a separate plane of existence that has broken through into Isthmus and is slowly bleeding into the physical world, converting land, creatures, and materials into its own form. They exist in the same space as Isthmus but are actively hostile and invasive, not neutral layers like Speculum. See [[1.8 Forces of Nature]] for the full mechanics of how these forces spread, convert creatures, and interact with each other.

- **The Corrupted Plane:** A dimension of solid purple crystal. Every surface is dark violet stone shot through with glowing veins. The air is still and silent. Nothing lives there that is not already corrupted. The plane presses against Isthmus constantly, and wherever the barrier is thin enough or has been broken, corruption leaks through and begins converting the physical world. Creatures fully consumed by corruption (10 stacks of Corrupt) are drawn into this plane upon death, their crystal statues becoming part of the landscape.
- **The Blighted Plane:** A dimension of endless rot. The ground is a deep layer of decaying organic matter and fungal growth, soft and spongy, releasing constant clouds of yellow spores. Everything is in a state of permanent decomposition that never finishes. There is no solid stone, no clean water, no fresh air. The plane is a single continuous fungal ecosystem that feeds on itself. Blight leaks into Isthmus through wounds in the land and spreads outward, rotting everything organic it touches.
- **The Infected Plane:** A dimension of green sickness. The entire plane is coated in mucus, filled with boils and pustules of every size, and shrouded in constant clouds of green gas. The terrain underneath the slime is normal stone and earth, unchanged, just buried. The plane does not destroy, it infects and possesses. Creatures fully consumed by infection (10 stacks of Infected) are not killed, they are permanently changed and become extensions of the Infected Plane's will, though they remain physically in Isthmus rather than being pulled into the plane itself.

Entry to any of the three corrupted planes is possible through high-level Corruption, Blight, or Infection magic, though no such spells currently exist in the handbook. A creature that enters one of these planes suffers the same environmental effects as being in a corrupted territory of that type: continuous CON saves against gaining stacks, damage over time, and the risk of permanent conversion. Exiting requires plane-shifting magic to return to Isthmus.
