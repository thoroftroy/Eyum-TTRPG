Every weapon, piece of armor, tool, and crafted item in Eyum is made from a material. The material determines the item's combat performance, its enchantment potential, and its market value. A Longsword forged from Iron is baseline; the same Longsword forged from Voidsteel is one of the most lethal weapons in existence. Materials come from many sources: mined from the earth, harvested from slain monsters, created through alchemical alloying, or purchased from merchants and smiths.

### Currency

Prices in the material tables are shown in two ways. The **Price** column is a multiplier: multiply an item's base price by this number to get the cost in the listed material (for example, a 1 Gold Iron Pickaxe made from Nerite would cost 1 Gold * 40 = 40 Gold). The **Price / lbs** column is the raw material cost per pound, used when selling unworked material or when the amount of material needed isn't tied to an item price. The material's listed currency reflects its actual cost: moderately valuable materials cost Gold per pound, while legendary materials cost Nerite per pound.

### Material Modifiers (The Five Core Stats)

Every material that can be used for weapons, armor, or tools has five stats. These are applied as flat bonuses or penalties on top of the item's own base stats:

| Modifier       | What It Does                                                                                                              |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| Damage         | Added to the weapon's base damage roll. Negative for poor materials, extremely high for legendary ones.                    |
| Accuracy       | Added to attack rolls made with the weapon.                                                                               |
| AC Bonus       | Added to the armor's base AC. For shields, this bonus is halved (rounded down). For non armor materials this is ignored.  |
| Tool Modifier  | Added to relevant skill checks when using a tool made from this material. Only applies if you are proficient with the tool. |
| Enchantability | Subtracted from the DC when inscribing runes onto the item with [[6.2 Runes]]. Higher is better; negative makes enchanting harder. |

For example, a standard Iron Plate chestpiece gives +8 AC and costs 6 Platinum. A Nerite Plate gives +8 + 6 (material AC Bonus) = +14 AC and costs 6 Platinum * 40 = 240 Platinum (24 Nerite).

Some materials have special properties beyond these five numbers: they may deal damage to the wielder, explode on water contact, change the damage type of a weapon, or grant resistance to an element when worn as armor. These properties are described in each material's Description column.

### How to Obtain Materials

**Mining:** Many metals and stones are extracted from the earth through traditional mining. Common materials like Iron, Copper, and Tin are widely available near most settlements. Rarer materials like Nerite, Orichalcum, and Mithril are found only in deep, dangerous deposits, often guarded by subterranean creatures.

**Harvesting from Monsters:** Slimes drop jars of usable mass (see [[2.6.2 Animal and Monster Materials#Slime Drops]]), Golems can be harvested for their entire body weight in material (see [[2.6.3 Special and Other Materials#Harvested]]), and Dragons provide scales, bones, and meat (see [[2.6.2 Animal and Monster Materials#Scales-Shells-Scutes]]). Nearly every monster in [[7.0 Monsters (WIP)]] drops some harvestable material. Characters with the Butcher archetype (see [[3.3 Leveling Up]]) recover more.

**Alchemy:** Alloys cannot be mined: they must be created by combining base metals through alchemical processes. Steel, Bronze, Electrum, Adamantine, Alnico, Duralumin, Pewter, Brass, and Voidsteel all require [[6.3 Alchemy]] to produce. Alloy stats vary based on the quality of the ingredients used, so their modifier ranges are shown in parentheses.

**Purchasing:** Most materials are available from specialist merchants. Smiths sell common metals, alchemists sell golem harvested goods, monster hunters sell animal parts, and jewelers sell gems. Prices per pound are listed in each table.

### Content Breakdown

#### [[2.6.1 Metals and Stones Materials]]

This file covers inorganic materials used primarily for forging weapons, armor, and metal tools. It is divided into three tables:

- **Standard Metals:** The primary forgeable materials. Every metal in this table has all five material modifiers plus a Price multiplier and a Price/lbs. Alloys (marked "Alloy, see 6.3 Alchemy") show stat ranges because their final values depend on ingredient quality.
	- **Name**, the material's name.
	- **Description**, physical appearance and special properties like reactivity or toxicity.
	- **Damage**, added to the weapon's base damage.
	- **Accuracy**, added to attack rolls.
	- **AC Bonus**, added to armor AC (halved for shields).
	- **Tool Modifier**, added to skill checks with tools made from this material.
	- **Enchantability**, subtracted from rune inscription DCs via [[6.2 Runes]].
	- **Price**, multiplier applied to an item's base price (alloy ranges in parentheses).
	- **Price / lbs**, raw material cost per pound.
	- **Obtained From**, source and how to acquire it.

- **Non Standard Metals:** Materials that are technically forgeable but have severe downsides: extreme brittleness, toxicity, reactivity with water, or forms that make them impractical (liquids and gases at room temperature). Uses the same columns as Standard Metals. These are rarely used for equipment but have niche alchemical applications.

- **Gems/Stones/Other:** Mineral materials for jewelry, alchemical ingredients, or special crafting. Some, like Solid Cloud, Solid Water, Voidsand, and Unstable Matter, have unique mechanical uses.
	- **Name**, the material's name.
	- **Source**, where it comes from.
	- **Usage**, what it does and how to use it.
	- **Price / lbs**, raw material cost per pound.

#### [[2.6.2 Animal and Monster Materials]]

This is the largest materials file, covering everything harvested from living creatures. It is organized into these tables:

- **Eggs:** Monster and animal eggs, eaten as food or incubated to hatch the source creature.
	- **Name**, the egg type.
	- **Source**, what creature lays it.
	- **Description**, physical appearance, size, and shell texture.
	- **Usage**, taste when eaten, incubation rules, special effects.
	- **Price / Unit**, cost per individual egg.

- **Meat:** Edible flesh from animals, monsters, and dragons. Common animal meat provides basic nutrition; rare monster and dragon meat grants special effects like temporary HP or long term sustenance.
	- **Name**, the meat type.
	- **Source**, what creature it comes from.
	- **Description**, taste, texture, and appearance.
	- **Usage**, rations provided, special effects when eaten, cooking requirements.
	- **Price / lbs**, cost per pound.

- **Oils/Blood/Glands:** Harvestable organs with alchemical or combat uses: venom glands, toxin glands, bile sacs, blood with magical properties, rendered fats, and lubricating oils.
	- **Name**, the organ or substance.
	- **Source**, what creature it comes from.
	- **Description**, physical appearance and properties.
	- **Usage**, how to apply, damage dealt, save DCs, number of doses.
	- **Price / lbs**, cost per pound when applicable.
	- **Price / Unit**, cost per organ or gland.

- **Eyes:** Aboleth eyes that grant temporary affinity bonuses and spell damage boosts when held. Each Aboleth variant provides a different elemental bonus. Multiple eyes do not stack.
	- **Name**, the eye type.
	- **Source**, which Aboleth variant it comes from.
	- **Usage**, affinity and damage bonuses, number of uses.
	- **Price / Unit**, cost per eye.

- **Hide/Skins/Fur:** Animal hides for crafting leather armor and clothing. Some monster hides grant elemental resistances (Cerberus Hide resists Fire, Yeti Hide resists Cold). Hides with full material stats can be used as armor materials; generic hides and furs provide only warmth and basic crafting applications.
	- **Name**, the hide type.
	- **Source**, what creature it comes from.
	- **Description**, texture, color, and special qualities.
	- **Usage**, what armor types it can make, resistances granted, special properties.
	- **AC Bonus**, added to armor AC.
	- **Enchantability**, subtracted from rune inscription DCs.
	- **Price (Mod)**, multiplier applied to item base price.
	- **Price / lbs**, raw hide cost per pound.

- **Scales/Shells/Scutes:** Dragon scales (organized alphabetically by element, each changing weapon damage type and granting elemental resistance), Bulette scales, Basilisk scales, and other reptilian scale types. Dragon scales are the most powerful crafting materials in the game, with the highest tier scales (Eldritch, Atomic, Corruption) exceeding even Voidsteel in modifiers.
	- **Name**, the scale type.
	- **Source**, what creature it comes from.
	- **Description**, physical appearance and special traits.
	- **Usage**, weapon effects, armor effects, damage type conversion.
	- **Damage**, added to weapon base damage.
	- **Accuracy**, added to attack rolls.
	- **AC Bonus**, added to armor AC.
	- **Tool Modifier**, added to skill checks with tools.
	- **Enchantability**, subtracted from rune inscription DCs.
	- **Price (Mod)**, multiplier applied to item base price.
	- **Price / lbs**, cost per pound.

- **Feathers/Mane/Hair/Wool:** Phoenix Feathers (constant warmth, affinity bonus), Thunderbird Feathers (affinity bonus by element), Roc Feathers (oversized fletching), and common animal feathers. Also includes mane hair for bowstrings, wool for warmth, and decorative materials.
	- **Name**, the material type.
	- **Source**, what creature it comes from.
	- **Description**, physical appearance and notable qualities.
	- **Usage**, crafting applications and mechanical bonuses.
	- **Price / lbs**, cost per pound.

- **Bones:** Skeletal materials including Dragon Bones and Gashadokuro Bones. Used for weapons (adding force damage dice), armor (granting saving throw bonuses), and tools.
	- **Name**, the bone type.
	- **Source**, what creature it comes from.
	- **Description**, density, color, and special qualities.
	- **Usage**, weapon and armor effects.
	- **Damage**, added to weapon base damage.
	- **Accuracy**, added to attack rolls.
	- **AC Bonus**, added to armor AC.
	- **Tool Modifier**, added to skill checks with tools.
	- **Enchantability**, subtracted from rune inscription DCs.
	- **Price (Mod)**, multiplier applied to item base price.
	- **Price / lbs**, cost per pound.

- **Talons/Claws/Horns:** Dense keratin and bone growths usable as dagger equivalents or Macuahuitl blades. Includes Roc Talons, Griffin Claws, Hydra Claws, unicorn horns (which double as wands), and common animal horns/tusks.
	- **Name**, the part type.
	- **Source**, what creature it comes from.
	- **Description**, size and hardness.
	- **Usage**, dagger stats when improvised, Macuahuitl compatibility, wand properties for horns.
	- **Damage**, added to weapon base damage.
	- **Accuracy**, added to attack rolls.
	- **Tool Modifier**, added to skill checks.
	- **Enchantability**, subtracted from rune inscription DCs.
	- **Price / lbs or Price / Unit**, cost per pound or per individual piece.

- **Fangs/Beaks:** Teeth and beaks from monsters and animals, usable as daggers or Macuahuitl blades. Some, like Basilisk Fangs and Anzu Fangs, carry venom or elemental damage. Uses the same columns as Talons/Claws/Horns.

- **Slime Drops:** Every slime type in [[7.3.1.1 Slimes]] drops 1d4 jars of mass when killed. Each variant has a unique effect: some are potions (Radiant heals, Lightning restores mana), some are hazardous (Atomic irradiates, Eldritch dissolves anything), and some are mundane. These are consumable items rather than crafting materials, though some are used in alchemy.
	- **Name**, what the jar contains.
	- **Source**, which slime variant it comes from.
	- **Usage**, what happens when you drink, touch, or throw it.
	- **Price**, cost per jar.

- **Misc/Other:** Unique harvestables that don't fit other categories: Phoenix Tears (massive spell amplification at the risk of destruction), unicorn meat, hippocampus gills for water breathing, griffin gizzard stones for enhanced cooking, and various single source oddities.

#### [[2.6.3 Special and Other Materials]]

This file covers crafted goods and golem harvested materials:

- **Manufactured:** Items you buy from shops or make yourself: Affinity Ink and Blank Runic Mediums (both used for rune crafting, see [[6.2 Runes#Making Your Own Runes]]) and Cloud Cloth (luxury fabric made from alchemically combined golem materials).
	- **Name**, the item's name.
	- **Source**, where to buy it or how to craft it.
	- **Usage**, what it does and how to use it.
	- **Price / lbs**, cost per pound.

- **Harvested:** Materials gathered from Golem kills (see [[7.3.1.2 Golems]]). These are powders, resins, and substances with potent effects: Golem Poison, Cursed Powder, Chaos Shrooms, Plague Sludge, Corrupt Resin (which can convert damage types), Hallowed Water (a full restore), and Gel (a powerful acid).
	- **Name**, the material's name.
	- **Source**, which golem produces it.
	- **Usage**, what it does and how to use it.
	- **Price / lbs**, cost per pound.
