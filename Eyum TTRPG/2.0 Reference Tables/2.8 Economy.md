### Currency
Eyum uses five coins, each worth 100 of the last. A single Copper is a meaningful amount of money: it buys a poor meal. A poor worker (farmhand, porter, unskilled laborer) earns 5-8 Copper for a full day's work. Items worth less than a single copper are typically bought in bulk or bartered for instead of purchased.

| Coin     | Conversion (Amount to next tier) | Reference                                                    | True Value (coins) |
| -------- | -------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------ |
| Copper   | 100                              | 5-8 Copper is a poor worker's daily wage                     | 1                  |
| Silver   | 100                              | About 2 weeks of a poor worker's wages                       | 100                |
| Gold     | 100                              | About 4-5 years of a poor worker's wages                     | 100,000            |
| Platinum | 100                              | About 450 years of a poor worker's wages                     | 100,000,000        |
| Nerite   | ---                              | A legendary sum, about 45,000 years of a poor worker's wages | 100,000,000,000    |

- **Coin weight:** 100 coins weigh about 1 lb (see Belt Pouch in [[2.5.4 Tools and Equipment]]). Carrying capacity is an optional rule, unless your GM says otherwise you always have room in your pack and enough strength to carry all of the coin which you have. 
- **Exchange:** Moneychangers and banks exist in larger settlements. They will exchange coins up or down freely, though GMs may add a small fee for exchanging. '
###### Increases and Decreases
Some skills or situations may change an items price or sell price by a percetage. This percentage is applied to the True Value of the item in coins. Convert all coins into their true value using the table, apply the percentage and then convert them back into regular coins using the highest value coins first. 
### Survival Costs
The cost of staying alive sets the floor for every wage in the world.

- A day of bare survival: 2 Poor Rations (2 Copper) + a floor spot at a poor inn (2 Copper) = **4 Copper/day**.
- A comfortable day (Quality Rations and a modest inn, see [[2.5.1 Supplies and Services]]): roughly 20-25 Copper.

### Starting Money by Background
Your starting money ([[Eyum TTRPG/3.0 Character Management/3.1 Building Your Character|3.1 Building Your Character]]) comes from your Background (see [[3.2 Character Information]]). Find the tier your background falls under, or pick the closest one with your GM:

| Tier | Starting Money | Example Backgrounds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Destitute | 20 Copper | Urchin |
| Poor | 1 Silver | Laborer, Hermit |
| Modest | 3 Silver | Acolyte, Sailor, Criminal |
| Comfortable | 6 Silver | Soldier, Scholar, Artisan |
| Wealthy | 1 Gold | Noble |
Your GM may adjust this up or down for custom backgrounds. A starting adventurer on a Comfortable budget can afford a decent weapon, light armor, and basic gear with a little left over. The GM may also allow you to start with some additional starting items based on your background outside of the starting money but that is on a case by case basis. 
### Buying and Selling
- **Listed prices are shop prices.** Every price in the handbook is what a merchant charges to sell the item to you.
- **Selling:** When you sell something, you get **50% of its listed price** by default. "Buyer's resale value" means the listed price, the amount the buyer can eventually sell it for.
- **Sell cap:** No skill or feat can push your sale price above **90% of the listed price**.
- **Enchanted items:** Shops charge an additional 50% markup on items with runes, on top of the runes' own price mods (see [[6.2 Runes]]).
- **Raw materials:** Price / lbs in [[2.6 Materials]] is the shop purchase price per pound of unworked material. Selling unworked material follows the 50% default rule above.
### What Players Should Expect to Make
Anchor on the mercenary ladder: Bodyguard 25 Copper/day, Mercenary 1 Silver/day, Skilled Mercenary 2 Silver/day, Super Mercenary 1 Gold/day. This is just a rough outline and will be on a case by case basis as determained by the GM. 

| Party level | Per player, per day of adventuring work | Typical quest payout (whole party) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1-3 | ~25 Copper | 1-3 Silver |
| 3-5 | ~1 Silver | 5-15 Silver |
| 6-10 | ~2 Silver | 20-60 Silver |
| 11+ | ~1 Gold | 3+ Gold |
> **Note**: Monster harvesting (see [[2.6.2 Animal and Monster Materials]]) pays on top of quest rewards and is usually where the real money is.
